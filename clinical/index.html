Project Confluence

Open-source in-silico research methods for questions that sit between haematology, surgical oncology, and pathology: adaptive therapy, clonal evolution, and the toxicity cost of pressure. This page is a 60-second read. It is not a clinic, not a trial dashboard, and not a treatment recommendation.

Computational research · MIT license · GitHub repository

The clinical problem this models

Continuous maximum-tolerated dosing can shrink measurable burden and, in the same motion, select resistant clones. Adaptive therapy asks whether modulating pressure can keep a sensitive population competitive enough to contain resistance — while host-health (marrow, mucosa, cardiac, immune competence) sets a hard toxicity budget. Those trade-offs are difficult to rehearse at the bedside. This repository is a computational sandbox for that argument, not a bedside tool.

What this repository is

A set of open-source in-silico research methods you can inspect, fork, and attack:

  • A mechanistic ODE tumor-microenvironment (burden, resistant clone, immune competence, stroma, host-health H).
  • Controller benchmarks — standard-of-care MTD versus Gatenby-style adaptive halt/resume versus connectome-style policies — on the same virtual patient.
  • An in-silico endpoint mapping layer: RECIST-like burden classes, CTCAE-like toxicity bands from host-health H, and Kaplan–Meier / log-rank / Cox on virtual cohorts. Mapped scores, not adjudicated imaging or a Phase I–III result.

Infusion commands are unitless and normalized. They are not a mg/kg regimen. TCGA-shaped scripts in this repo still use synthetic patients until real GDC extracts exist; there is no completed TCGA clinical validation here.

What a clinician can do with it

  1. Critique the assumptions. Growth rates, phenotypic switching, immune kill, PK/PD class half-lives, and what “host-health” is allowed to stand in for.
  2. Suggest assays that would falsify the model. Serial ctDNA or fusion-allele fraction, immune-competence markers, and toxicity readouts that should move if the ODE were describing a real patient.
  3. Mentor a DE Medicine student. Walk the closed loop, demand honesty about what is simulated, and treat the codebase as a teaching object — not a product to deploy on a ward.

The research loop (four steps)

Same loop the scientific modules run. Nothing here is a clinical order.

  1. 1

    Observation

    Noisy, partial measurements: burden, resistance proxies, immune competence, host-health. Not a complete EHR and not a pathology report.

  2. 2

    Decision

    A research controller proposes a unitless policy U(t) — MTD, adaptive, or connectome-style. A hypothesis about pressure, not a prescription.

  3. 3

    Simulated infusion

    First-order PK/PD into the ODE. Concentrations are simulation-scaled class references, not a dosing protocol.

  4. 4

    Mapped endpoints

    RECIST-like classes, toxicity bands, Kaplan–Meier on virtual cohorts. Research scores for scrutiny — not a trial readout.

What this is not

  • Not a medical device, diagnostic, or clinical decision support system.
  • Not a treatment planner, dosing engine, or path to a clinical outcome.
  • Not FDA / EMA cleared, and not a Phase I / II / III result.
  • Not a completed TCGA (GDC) retrospective clinical validation.
  • Not a claim of cure, disease eradication, or ward-ready software.

The ask: mentorship, critique, falsification

If you teach or practice at the University of Uyo teaching hospital (or a similar service), the useful next step is not endorsement.
